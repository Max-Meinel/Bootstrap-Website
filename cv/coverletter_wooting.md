**What makes you interested in working at Wooting?**

I've been a gamer for most of my life, and Wooting keyboards were one of those products that genuinely changed how I think about hardware. The analog input, the rapid trigger precision, the attention to how the software actually feels to use. I didn't just buy it, I kept reading about how it worked, why the decisions were made, and how Wootility tied it all together. That kind of product doesn't come from people who just ship features. It comes from people who care deeply about the intersection of hardware and software performance.

That philosophy resonates with me beyond gaming too. I fly FPV drones in my free time, where hardware and software are inseparable: the flight controller firmware, the radio link latency, the configurator UI all have to work in perfect sync. Milliseconds matter and there's no hiding behind abstractions. I'm drawn to contexts where performance isn't just a nice-to-have but the entire point.

Wootility specifically excites me because it's a browser-based app that talks directly to hardware, where clean UI and real hardware interaction live side by side. That's a genuinely interesting technical space, and I want to be part of it.

**Can you share one recent project you are proud of?**

I co-founded NEXtickets, a ticketing platform for small clubs and event organizers. Together with a small team of developers, we built it from scratch with Next.js, TypeScript, and PostgreSQL, covering event creation, ticket sales, and on-site validation. What I'm most proud of is that it's a real product with real users, not a portfolio exercise. Every decision had to hold up under actual use.

Another one is an AI chat moderation model I built for a close friend who is a well-known Twitch moderator. I trained a multi-task classifier based on XLM-RoBERTa that predicts severity and category, with an LLM-as-a-Judge layer for edge cases. The ML model acts as a fast pre-filter, the LLM handles the rest. It's still in fine-tuning, but he may start using it in his first channels soon. False positives are critical here since a wrong ban is a bad experience, so getting the precision right matters more than anything.
